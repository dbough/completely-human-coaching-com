Before We Start

What to Expect When Working With Me

If you've never worked with a coach before, it's fair to wonder what actually happens once you get on a call. This page walks through it — plainly, without the jargon — so you know what you're stepping into before you ever book a session.

What Coaching Is

A conversation, not a consultation

Coaching is a partnership — a series of conversations that help you understand yourself better: what you actually want, what's been getting in the way, and how you want to move forward. I'm not there to hand you answers or tell you what to do. I'm there to think alongside you, ask questions that matter, and help you hear yourself more clearly.

How I Coach

I coach the person, not just the problem

Whatever you bring to a session, I'm paying attention to more than the surface issue. A few things that shape how I show up:

  • I trust that you already have what you need inside you — my job is to help you find it, not supply it
  • I care about the whole person in front of me, not just the problem they walked in with
  • I pay attention to what's happening right now in the conversation, not just what you're describing
  • Sometimes the shift isn't "solving" something — it's seeing yourself or your situation differently
  • You stay the authority on your own life. I won't tell you what's right for you

A Session

What a coaching session looks like

You bring something you want to explore. It doesn't need to be a fully formed goal or a tidy problem statement — most of the time, it isn't. Something like:

  • “I don't know if I want this job anymore.”
  • “Something feels off.”
  • “I know what I should do, but I'm not doing it.”
  • “I'm stuck between two choices.”
  • “I don't know what I want next.”

From there, the shape of a session is loose, not a formula:

  1. Bringsomething on your mind, even half-formed
  2. Explorewhat's underneath it — patterns, values, emotions, what's really going on
  3. Discoversomething you hadn't quite seen before
  4. Decidewhat you want to take with you from the conversation

We follow what's actually emerging, not a script. If the conversation goes somewhere more useful than where we started, we go there.

Things We Might Try

Sometimes we talk. Sometimes we experiment.

Depending on where the conversation goes, we might:

  • Explore your values, and where your life does or doesn't line up with them
  • Look at where you feel genuinely fulfilled, and where you don't
  • Try on a different perspective, just to see how it fits
  • Notice a limiting belief or an inner critic that's been running the show
  • Picture a future version of yourself, or imagine what a wiser part of you would say
  • Use a metaphor or image that captures something words alone can't
  • Notice what's happening in your body, not just your head
  • Change positions — literally get up and move if it helps something shift
  • Brainstorm possibilities without judging them yet
  • Design a small experiment to try before we talk again
  • Just sit with a question, instead of rushing to answer it

Written down, some of that can sound unusual. In practice, nothing is forced. If something doesn't land for you, we drop it and try something else.

What Coaching Isn't

How this is different from other kinds of support

  • Therapy. Coaching isn't therapy. We may talk about the past, emotions, or hard experiences, but we're generally exploring them in service of the present and where you're headed, not diagnosing or treating something. If deeper therapeutic work would serve you better, I'll say so.
  • Consulting. I'm not here to diagnose your situation and hand you a prescription. I don't have your answer — you do.
  • Mentoring. My own experience might come up when it's relevant, but the goal isn't to teach you to follow the path I took. Yours will look different.
  • Advice. I may offer an observation, a challenge, or a perspective — but coaching isn't me telling you what to do. You stay responsible for your own choices.

Between Sessions

The work doesn't always end when the call does

A session might end with an action, a small experiment, something to notice, a conversation to have, a question to sit with, or a commitment — or sometimes nothing formal at all. The point isn't homework for its own sake. It's giving what came up in the conversation a chance to show up in your actual life.

What I Ask From You

Bring yourself, and something you're willing to explore

You don't need a polished goal or a life plan already worked out. Openness, curiosity, and honesty matter more than having the right words. You're responsible for your own decisions — I'm just here to help you think them through.

“I don't know, but something needs to change” is a perfectly good place to start.

Questions

A few things people usually want to know

What should I talk about?

Whatever is actually on your mind — a decision, a feeling that something's off, a pattern you keep hitting. There's no required topic. We start with what's real for you right now.

What if I don't know what I want to work on?

That's a completely normal place to start. Not knowing is something we can explore together — you don't need clarity before the first session, that's often part of what coaching helps with.

Will you give me advice?

Not usually, and not by default. I might offer an observation or a perspective if it seems useful, but the goal is to help you find your own answer, not hand you mine.

Is coaching confidential?

Yes. What we talk about stays between us.

How is this different from therapy?

Therapy often looks at healing and mental health, sometimes rooted in the past. Coaching is generally more forward-looking — focused on where you want to go and what's in the way, with someone who assumes you're not broken, just stuck or unclear. If something comes up that would be better served by therapy, I'll say so directly.

How often should we meet, and how many sessions will I need?

It varies by person. Some people come for a handful of sessions around a specific decision; others meet regularly for ongoing support. We can figure out a rhythm that fits what you're working through, and adjust as we go.

What if an exercise or technique doesn't feel right for me?

We drop it, no explanation needed. Nothing here is one-size-fits-all, and you always know better than any technique does.
